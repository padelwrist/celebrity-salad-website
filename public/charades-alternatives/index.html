Games like Charades

Charades alternatives for when acting alone is getting old.

If you like fast guessing games but want a different clue mechanic, there are plenty of party games that keep the same team energy without simply repeating another round of Charades.

10 games like Charades

  1. Celebrity Salad
  2. Fishbowl / Salad Bowl
  3. Reverse charades
  4. One-word guessing
  5. Pictionary-style drawing
  6. Who Am I?
  7. Forbidden-word clues
  8. Sound effects only
  9. Rapid-fire descriptions
  10. Telephone Pictionary
The easiest switchIf your group likes Charades but wants more variety, choose a game that changes the clue rule while keeping teams, a timer and quick guesses. You get familiar energy without playing the exact same format.

1. Celebrity Salad

Celebrity Salad starts with verbal clues rather than acting. Players add famous names, split into two teams and guess the same set of celebrities across three rounds.

The first round allows description, the second allows one clue word and the third is acting only. That means Charades becomes the finale rather than the entire game.

The repeated names create a memory layer too. By the acting round, a tiny gesture can be enough because everybody remembers what happened when the same celebrity appeared earlier.

Read the full Celebrity Salad rules.

2. Fishbowl or Salad Bowl

Fishbowl and Salad Bowl are closely related to Celebrity. Players write words, names or phrases, then reuse the same pool across multiple rounds with different clue restrictions.

House rules differ, but the core appeal is the same: the game gets funnier as the group becomes more familiar with the possible answers. See our Salad Bowl game guide.

3. Reverse charades

Turn normal Charades around. One player guesses while the rest of their team acts the prompt together.

This is a good option if people dislike being the only person performing. The group shares the awkwardness, and several people often attempt completely different interpretations at once.

4. One-word guessing

Instead of acting, the clue-giver says a single word. Their team then tries to identify the answer. You can play with celebrities, films, objects or any shared prompt list.

The restriction makes clue choice important, but the game stays accessible to people who would rather speak than mime.

5. Drawing games

Replace acting with drawing. One player sketches while teammates guess before time runs out. You do not need artistic ability. In most groups, an unrecognisable drawing produces more laughs than a perfect one.

6. Who Am I?

Each player gets a person or character they cannot see. They ask yes-or-no questions to work out their identity.

It is calmer than Charades, works well around a table and does not need teams unless you want to make it competitive.

7. Forbidden-word clues

Give the clue-giver an answer plus a few obvious words they are not allowed to say. Their team has to guess while the clue-giver finds a less direct route.

This keeps the fast verbal pressure of a guessing game while removing the performance element completely.

8. Sound effects only

Allow noises but no words or gestures. It sits somewhere between description and Charades and works particularly well for animals, films, objects and actions.

9. Rapid-fire descriptions

Give a player a stack of names or words and one minute to get through as many as possible using normal verbal descriptions. It is straightforward, competitive and easy to teach.

If you then reuse the same prompts with tighter rules, you are effectively moving towards the Celebrity or Fishbowl format.

10. Telephone Pictionary

Each person writes a phrase, passes it on for someone to draw, then the next person writes what they think the drawing represents. Continue alternating between writing and drawing until the chain returns to the start.

It is less frantic than Charades but produces the same kind of escalating misunderstanding.

Which Charades alternative should you choose?

  • Want more team competition? Try Celebrity Salad or Fishbowl.
  • Want less pressure on one person? Try reverse charades.
  • Prefer talking to acting? Use one-word clues or forbidden-word descriptions.
  • Prefer something quieter? Choose Who Am I? or Telephone Pictionary.
  • Have a big group? Pick a timed two-team format and rotate clue-givers.

Why Celebrity Salad works as a Charades alternative

Celebrity Salad keeps the strongest parts of Charades: quick guesses, physical comedy, teams and a timer. But it delays the acting until the final round.

That gives people who prefer verbal clues two rounds to participate before anyone needs to mime. It also makes the acting round easier and funnier because every answer has already appeared twice.
