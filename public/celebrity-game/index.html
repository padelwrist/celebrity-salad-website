Party game guide

The Celebrity game.

A simple team game with one clever twist: everyone keeps guessing the same famous names while the clue rules get harder.

What is the Celebrity game?

The Celebrity game, sometimes called Celebrities or the Name Game, is a team-based guessing game. Players create the content themselves by writing down famous names, then teams race to guess those names before the timer runs out.

What makes it different from a normal round of charades is repetition. The same set of names comes back in later rounds. The clues get more restrictive, but the group gradually learns the whole pool, so memory and shared jokes become part of the game.

The basic ideaEveryone adds names. Two teams alternate timed turns. Each correct guess scores a point. When every name has been guessed, all the names return and the next clue style begins.

How to set up the Celebrity game

  1. Choose the players. Four people is a workable minimum, with larger groups split into two roughly even teams.
  2. Create the name pool. Ask every player to contribute a few celebrities or recognisable characters.
  3. Keep submissions secret. The fun is better when players do not know the full set before Round One.
  4. Choose a turn time. Shorter turns feel frantic; longer turns give clue-givers more time to recover from difficult names.
  5. Start with description. Teams alternate until every celebrity has been guessed.

The three rounds

Round 1: Describe the celebrity

The clue-giver can describe the person using words, but cannot say the celebrity's name or a part of it. Once the team gets the answer, the clue-giver moves straight to the next name.

Round 2: One word

Return every name to the pool. For each celebrity, the clue-giver can say one clue word only. The team now has the advantage of remembering the names and descriptions from Round One.

Round 3: Act it

Return the names again. This time the clue-giver acts or gestures without speaking. By now the same celebrity may have developed a memorable clue, gesture or in-joke that the team can recognise quickly.

How does scoring work?

A simple scoring system works best: one point for every correctly guessed name. Keep a running total across all rounds and declare the highest-scoring team the winner at the end.

House rules vary on passing, penalties and the order of the final two rounds. Celebrity Salad uses a clear default of Describe It → One Word → Act It, with skipped names remaining in play and no automatic penalty for skipping.

Why does the game get funnier each round?

The first round gives everyone context. A name that seems difficult initially may become obvious later because the group remembers a distinctive story, clue or failed attempt from earlier in the game.

That shared memory is the real engine of the Celebrity game. The one-word and acting rounds look more restrictive on paper, but often become faster because the room already knows the possible answers.

What names work best?

A balanced pool beats a list of only the biggest stars. Choose people and characters with enough recognition that most players have a chance, but enough personality that clue-givers can do something interesting with them.

  • Actors and TV personalities
  • Musicians and performers
  • Sportspeople
  • Historical figures
  • Fictional characters
  • Well-known internet or cultural figures

Playing without paper slips

The traditional game needs scraps of paper, pens, a bowl and someone to keep score. Celebrity Salad moves that admin onto one iPhone: players add names privately, the app manages teams, timers, scores and the returning pool of celebrities, while the group still plays together in the same room.

The core idea remains the same. The app is there to remove setup friction, not replace the social part of the game.
