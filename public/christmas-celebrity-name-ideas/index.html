CHRISTMAS NAME IDEAS

60 festive names for the Salad.

Christmas film characters, musicians, familiar faces and easy festive wildcards for Celebrity, Fishbowl, Salad Bowl or Charades.

How to use these Christmas name ideas

A festive name pool should still work as a guessing game. The best prompts are recognisable across the room, distinctive enough to describe and memorable enough to return in later rounds.

For Celebrity Salad, mix these suggestions with names your group submits privately. You do not need every name to be Christmassy. A mostly normal celebrity pool with 20–30% festive names usually feels more natural than sixty versions of Santa.

A balanced festive SaladStart with familiar names, add a few Christmas-specific characters and performers, then let the group supply the surprises.

Christmas Charades prompts beyond celebrity names

If you are playing ordinary Charades rather than Celebrity Salad, you can widen the pool to actions and objects. Good prompts include decorating a Christmas tree, wrapping a present, pulling a cracker, building a snowman, carving a turkey, ice skating, hanging a stocking, opening a difficult parcel and untangling fairy lights.

Celebrity Salad itself is designed around public figures and recognisable names, so keep the in-app Salad focused on people and characters rather than generic objects.

How hard should the names be?

60% easy

People or characters almost everyone knows.

30% medium

Recognisable once someone gives the right clue.

10% wild

Funny curveballs rather than impossible trivia.

A festive game often spans more generations than an ordinary party. That makes difficulty balance more important, not less. A 17-year-old and a 70-year-old do not need identical pop-culture knowledge if the pool gives both of them enough footholds.

Five ways to make the festive pool better

  1. Mix eras. Use modern names, older music and familiar film characters.
  2. Avoid a single franchise. Ten characters from one film makes the game repetitive.
  3. Think about acting. Distinctive voices, jobs and mannerisms pay off in Act It.
  4. Let the group contribute. Private submissions create better surprises than a completely prewritten list.
  5. Keep it inclusive. Christmas flavour should not require religious knowledge to play.
