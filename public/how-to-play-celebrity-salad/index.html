How to play

Celebrity Salad rules.

Add names to the Salad, split into two teams and play the same celebrities through three increasingly restrictive rounds.

Celebrity Salad in 30 seconds

Celebrity Salad is a team guessing game for an in-person group. Every player secretly adds celebrity names. Those names form the Salad and are reused in every round. Teams alternate timed turns, scoring one point for every correct guess.

The classic game has three rounds: Describe It, One Word and Act It. The same celebrity names return each time, so memory becomes part of the game as well as clue-giving.

What you needAt least four players works best, one iPhone running Celebrity Salad, and enough famous names that most of the room will recognise.

1. Set up the game

  1. Create a Salad. Choose your turn length, number of celebrity submissions per player and the rounds you want to play.
  2. Add the players. Enter everyone taking part. Nicknames are completely fine.
  3. Split into two teams. Celebrity Salad can assign the teams, and you can adjust them before the game begins.
  4. Add celebrity names privately. Pass the phone around so each player can add their own names without showing the rest of the group.
  5. Toss the Salad. Once everyone has submitted, the names are mixed and the game is ready to begin.

2. Round One: Describe It

The clue-giver sees one celebrity at a time and describes that person to their team. You can use as many words as you like, but you cannot say the celebrity's name or any part of it.

When your team guesses correctly, tap Correct and move to the next name. If you cannot use a name, tap Skip. The skipped celebrity stays available and can appear again later.

When the timer reaches zero, the turn ends and play moves to the other team. Continue alternating until every celebrity in the Salad has been guessed. Each correct answer is worth one point.

3. Round Two: One Word

Every celebrity from Round One goes back into the Salad. This time the clue-giver may say exactly one clue word for each celebrity. No extra explanation and no gestures.

This is where paying attention earlier matters. Everyone has already heard all the celebrity names once, so a well-chosen single word can trigger the memory of a clue from Round One.

4. Round Three: Act It

All the names return again for the final classic round. The clue-giver can act and gesture, but cannot speak or make clue sounds. Your team tries to connect the performance with names they remember from the earlier rounds.

Turns keep alternating until the Salad is empty for the final time.

5. Scoring and winning

Every correct celebrity is worth one point to the guessing team. Scores carry across all three rounds. At the end of the final round, the team with the highest total score wins.

If you accidentally mark the wrong celebrity as correct, Celebrity Salad includes Undo so you can put the point and celebrity back without disrupting the game.

What makes a good celebrity name?

The best names are recognisable to most of the room but still give people something interesting to describe or act. A good Salad usually mixes obvious names with a few harder choices.

  • Choose people most of the group has heard of.
  • Mix actors, musicians, sportspeople, presenters, historical figures and fictional characters.
  • Avoid deliberately obscure names that only one player could know.
  • Inside jokes can be fun, but agree as a group whether non-celebrities are allowed.

How many people can play?

Two teams need at least two people each, so four is a practical minimum. The game becomes more varied as the group gets larger because there are more clue-givers, more celebrity submissions and more shared memories to work with.

For very large groups, reduce the number of submissions per player or shorten each turn so the overall game stays moving.
