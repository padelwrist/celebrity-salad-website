FAMILY CHRISTMAS GAMES

Christmas games for the whole family.

Games that still work when children, parents, grandparents and the relative who claims not to like games are all in the same room.

What makes a Christmas game work for mixed ages?

The hardest part of a family Christmas game is not the rules. It is choosing references and skills that do not give one generation a permanent advantage. The best games let knowledge transfer between people, use teams and keep eliminated players to a minimum.

For a mixed-age roomChoose games where knowing one answer helps everyone with the next. That is why repeated-name games, team drawing and simple music rounds work so well.

10 easy family Christmas games

1. Celebrity Salad

Celebrity Salad is especially good for mixed generations because the same names return in every round. Someone who did not know a celebrity at the start can learn them during Describe It and recognise them later in One Word or Act It.

Ask everyone to submit people or characters the whole family has a reasonable chance of knowing. Mix current names with older celebrities, presenters, musicians and familiar fictional characters. The game supports 4–12 players and two to four teams on one shared iPhone or iPad. See the Christmas name ideas or the official rules.

2. Christmas Charades

Use films, songs, traditions and easy actions. Give younger players simpler prompts and let teammates help with reading if needed. Keep turns short so the game feels energetic rather than intimidating.

3. Festive Pictionary

Drawing levels the room surprisingly well. Use prompts such as Christmas tree, sleigh, snowman, mince pie, reindeer and cracker. Nobody needs to draw well; in fact, poor drawings usually improve the game.

4. Christmas music generations

Build rounds by decade so every age group has a moment: older classics, 1980s and 1990s favourites, 2000s songs and newer tracks. Award team points rather than individual points.

5. Family Who Am I?

Use famous people and characters everyone has heard of. Players ask yes-or-no questions until they guess the name on their card. It works well before dinner because people can join at different times.

6. Christmas memory tray

Place festive objects on a tray, show them for 30 seconds, then cover them. Teams write down everything they remember. Use physical objects from around the house so the game needs almost no preparation.

7. Guess the Christmas film

Read a deliberately vague one-sentence plot description and let teams identify the film. Keep the list familiar rather than obscure, and accept alternative titles where appropriate.

8. Festive categories

Go around naming Christmas foods, songs, films, decorations or winter clothing without repeats. Younger players can be given a few extra seconds. Once answers become difficult, move to a new category rather than dragging the round out.

9. What's in the stocking?

Put ordinary household or festive objects into a stocking or bag. Players feel one item without looking and try to identify it. Use safe objects with no sharp edges and avoid anything messy.

10. Christmas story chain

One person starts a story with a sentence. Each person adds one sentence until the story reaches a ridiculous conclusion. Give the group three required words, such as “turkey”, “sleigh” and “neighbour”, if it needs structure.

Adapting games for different ages

Young children

Use visual prompts, characters and simple actions. Avoid trivia that depends on dates or detailed cultural knowledge. Pair younger players with older teammates rather than giving them a separate version of the game.

Teenagers

Let them contribute current celebrities, musicians and online personalities while keeping the overall pool broad enough for the adults. Mixed submissions are usually funnier than an adults-versus-kids split.

Older relatives

Include recognisable names from earlier decades and avoid making speed the only skill. Team formats give people time to contribute without putting one person under pressure.

Building a Christmas Celebrity Salad for the family

A good family Salad should look deliberately mixed. If every name comes from one generation, later rounds become easy for half the room and frustrating for the other half.

  • About half the pool should be people or characters nearly everyone knows.
  • Add names from several decades of music, film, sport and television.
  • Use a few festive characters as easy anchors.
  • Keep niche internet personalities or historical figures to a minority.
  • Let each person contribute privately so nobody can curate the whole game around their own interests.

Best games after Christmas dinner

After dinner, favour games that can start from sofas and chairs. Celebrity Salad, music intros, Who Am I?, memory games and categories all work without clearing a table or moving furniture. Save louder drawing or acting games for later if the room wants more energy.
