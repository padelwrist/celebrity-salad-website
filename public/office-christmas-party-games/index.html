OFFICE CHRISTMAS PARTY GAMES

Work Christmas games without the cringe.

Easy team games that get colleagues talking without forced speeches, drinking rules or making the new starter perform alone in front of the boss.

What makes a good office Christmas party game?

A work party is different from a night with close friends. People may span several age groups, departments and levels of seniority, and not everyone wants to perform. The safest games have simple rules, team participation and no requirement to reveal personal information.

The work-party testA good game should make joining easier than opting out. Teams, short rounds and familiar references usually beat anything built around embarrassment.
  • No drinking required.
  • No jokes at one colleague's expense.
  • No specialist knowledge of company gossip.
  • No long individual performances.
  • No elimination that leaves half the room standing around.

10 office Christmas party games

1. Celebrity Salad

For teams of 4–12, Celebrity Salad is an easy centrepiece game. Colleagues privately submit famous names, then two to four teams guess the same pool through Describe It, One Word and Act It. The repetition gives people something shared to laugh about without requiring anyone to tell a personal story.

For work groups, ask for broadly recognisable celebrities and public figures rather than niche in-jokes. One shared iPhone or iPad handles timing and scores. The custom classic game is free. Read the official rules.

2. Christmas music intros

Play short intros and let teams name the song and artist. Use a mix of decades so the quiz does not accidentally become a test of age. Ten tracks is usually enough for a useful round.

3. Festive team trivia

Keep it broad: Christmas films, food, music, general knowledge and a small amount of company-safe trivia. Teams reduce pressure and stop the loudest quiz person from dominating.

4. Christmas reverse Charades

One person guesses while the rest of their team acts. Because several people perform together, it feels much less exposing than traditional Charades and creates instant group energy.

5. The Price Is Festive

Show a set of ordinary festive products and let teams guess their prices. Closest answer gets the point. It works well around tables and requires almost no explanation.

6. Christmas Pictionary

Give each team a whiteboard or paper. Use neutral festive prompts rather than company references. One person draws while teammates guess against a 45- or 60-second timer.

7. Guess the desk

Before the event, collect optional close-up photographs of harmless desk details: a mug, keyboard sticker, plant or novelty item. Teams guess who each one belongs to. Only use contributions people volunteered.

8. Festive categories

Teams take turns naming items in a category without repeats: Christmas films, winter foods, things in a stocking or festive songs. Add a five-second limit once the obvious answers disappear.

9. Christmas bingo

Create cards with things likely to happen during the event rather than personal traits: someone mentions the weather, a Christmas jumper appears, somebody takes a group photo. It runs quietly in the background while people mingle.

10. Mini awards with safe categories

Use categories that celebrate rather than embarrass: best problem-solver, calmest under pressure, most likely to know the answer, best desk snack supplier. Avoid appearance, relationships, alcohol and anything connected to performance reviews.

Which games work for different office sizes?

4 to 8 people

Celebrity Salad, Pictionary, music intros and categories all keep everyone involved. With a smaller Celebrity Salad group, use four or five names per person.

9 to 12 people

Celebrity Salad is still a good fit and supports up to 12 players. Two or three teams keeps turns moving without fragmenting the room too much.

13 to 30 people

Use games where many people can participate at once: trivia, music intros, reverse Charades or bingo. If the group wants Celebrity Salad, run separate heats or smaller Salads rather than exceeding the supported player count.

30+ people

Think in tables or teams. One central host can run music, trivia or a visual round while each table submits one answer. Avoid anything that requires every individual to take a turn.

How to keep office games inclusive

Christmas events can include people who do not celebrate Christmas personally, people who do not drink, remote colleagues, new starters and senior leaders. The easiest approach is to make the event festive without making participation depend on a particular belief or tradition.

  • Use optional team participation rather than calling people out individually.
  • Keep content workplace-safe and culturally broad.
  • Choose games that work without alcohol.
  • Do not use secrets, dating history or “most likely to” questions that could become personal.
  • Keep rounds short enough that people can leave a conversation and rejoin naturally.

A simple 45-minute office games plan

  1. 10 minutes: Christmas music intros while people settle in.
  2. 25 minutes: one fast Celebrity Salad game or several short team rounds.
  3. 10 minutes: festive trivia or a quick final.

You do not need to fill the whole event. Games work best as a reason to mix the room, not as a timetable everyone has to obey.
