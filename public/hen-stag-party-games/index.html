WEEKEND AWAY

Hen and stag party games people will actually join in with.

Games that help different friendship groups mix, without making one person perform for the room all night.

What makes a good hen or stag game?

A hen or stag group often contains people from different parts of someone's life: school friends, family, work friends and partners who have barely met. The best games create shared moments without assuming everyone already has the same in-jokes.

Good weekend-game testCan a new person understand it in under a minute, join without being embarrassed, and enjoy it even if they are not drinking? If yes, you are probably safe.

Choose games for the moment

When people first arrive

Use light, low-pressure formats: Who Am I?, a simple quiz about the bride or groom, human bingo or Two Truths and a Lie. The goal is mixing, not winning.

After dinner or back at the accommodation

This is the sweet spot for a bigger team game such as Celebrity Salad, Charades or Pictionary. Everyone is in one place and the group has warmed up enough to be louder.

Late in the evening

Rules need to get simpler, not more complicated. Rapid categories, one-word clues and short team rounds work better than anything with a long explanation.

Eight ideas that work well

1. Celebrity Salad

Each person privately submits celebrity names, so everybody contributes something to the game before anyone starts guessing. Two to four teams then play the same names through Describe It, One Word and Act It.

It works particularly well for mixed groups because nobody needs to know private stories about the bride or groom. The room creates its own in-jokes as the same celebrities reappear. See the official rules.

2. Who Am I?

Give everyone a famous person or character they cannot see and let them ask yes-or-no questions. Easy to run while people are still arriving.

3. Team Pictionary

Short drawing rounds create energy without asking people to act alone. Keep prompts broad and recognisable.

4. The couple quiz

Ask light questions about the couple and let teams guess the answers. Better when it is affectionate rather than designed to catch someone out.

5. Reverse Charades

The whole team acts for one guesser, which spreads the awkwardness in a good way.

6. Photo scavenger list

Give teams a short list of harmless photos to collect during the day. Keep it achievable and avoid involving strangers who have not agreed to participate.

7. Rapid categories

Pick categories linked loosely to the weekend, then alternate answers against a timer. No prep and no props.

8. Mini trivia rounds

Let different friendship groups contribute a few questions about topics they know. This spreads ownership and stops one group dominating the evening.

What to avoid

  • Humiliation as the whole mechanic. One person being the joke gets old quickly.
  • Compulsory drinking. Keep alcohol separate from the rules so everyone can participate.
  • Private-history quizzes only one friendship group can answer. They can make half the room feel like spectators.
  • Long elimination. A weekend group should stay together, not spend an hour watching the final three people play.
  • Rules that need props you forgot to pack. Low-prep wins.

How many people?

For 6–12 people, most team guessing games work with almost no adjustment. Above 12, shorten turns and use clear teams. For a genuinely big weekend group, see our large-group games guide.
