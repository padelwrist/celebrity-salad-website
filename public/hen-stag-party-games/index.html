Hen & stag party games

Group games without the awkward icebreaker energy.

The best hen and stag party games bring different friendship groups together quickly. They are easy to explain, do not depend on embarrassing one person and still work whether people are drinking or not.

Quick picks for hen and stag groups

  • Best for mixed friendship groups: Celebrity Salad.
  • Best with no preparation: Charades or Who Am I?
  • Best for learning stories about the group: two truths and a lie or a couple quiz.
  • Best for 12+ people: two-team guessing games or reverse charades.
  • Best for a quieter first night: themed trivia or a photo challenge.
Keep the guest of honour in the gameA good hen or stag game should not turn one person into the target for the whole evening. Games are usually funnier when everyone has something to do.

1. Celebrity Salad

Celebrity Salad works particularly well for hen and stag weekends because the group creates the content. Everyone privately adds famous names, then the whole pool is played three times using different clue rules.

Round One is description, Round Two allows one clue word and Round Three is acting only. Because the same celebrities keep returning, the game develops its own references and running jokes as the night goes on.

You can make the pool fit the group without turning it into a private in-joke nobody understands. Mix obvious celebrities with people connected to favourite films, reality shows, sports, music or shared eras.

More easy hen and stag party games

2. Who Am I?

Give each person a celebrity or character they cannot see. Players ask yes-or-no questions until they identify themselves. It is simple enough to play while people are arriving.

3. Reverse charades

One person guesses while the rest of the team acts the prompt together. It is louder and less exposing than normal charades because nobody has to perform alone.

4. The story behind the photo

Ask guests to submit an old photo involving the bride, groom or guest of honour. Show them one at a time and let the group guess the story, location or year.

5. Couple trivia

Prepare a short set of questions about the couple and play in teams. Keep the questions warm and funny rather than using the format to embarrass anyone.

6. Rapid-fire categories

Choose categories relevant to the weekend such as songs from a particular decade, holiday destinations, films, footballers or reality TV stars. Go around the room without repeating an answer.

7. Team drawing

Use prompts linked to the group, the destination or general pop culture. Bad drawings are usually better entertainment than accurate ones.

8. Memory lane

Each guest writes a short memory involving the guest of honour. Read them anonymously and let the group guess who wrote each one.

Games for a group where people do not all know each other

Hen and stag weekends often combine school friends, work friends, siblings, cousins and partners. Avoid games that depend entirely on shared history at the start of the weekend.

A team guessing game works well because it creates interaction without demanding personal disclosure. Put people who know each other less well on the same teams and rotate the clue-giver so everybody gets a turn.

Once the group has warmed up, more personal formats such as memory games and couple trivia tend to land better.

Games for a large hen or stag party

For twelve people or more, use teams and timers. Games that require each person to complete a separate long challenge can become slow very quickly.

  • Split into two balanced teams.
  • Use turns of around 30 to 60 seconds.
  • Rotate the active player instead of asking for volunteers every time.
  • Avoid elimination so nobody spends half the night watching.
  • Use one host or one shared phone to keep the score and pace clear.

See our full guide to games for large groups for more options.

Do hen and stag games need to involve drinking?

No. Drinking can be part of the event without being built into the rules. Making alcohol optional means drivers, non-drinkers and anyone taking a break can take part without a separate version of the game.

The easiest way to create energy is time pressure, team competition and familiar references. Those mechanics work whether there is a drink in anyone's hand or not.

Choosing celebrities for the group

For a hen or stag group, aim for names that produce a reaction but remain broadly guessable. A balanced pool might include pop stars, reality personalities, actors, footballers, TV characters and nostalgic names from the decade the group grew up in.

Do not make every entry an obscure private reference. A few difficult names are funny; an entire bowl of them is work. Our celebrity name ideas page is a useful starting point.
