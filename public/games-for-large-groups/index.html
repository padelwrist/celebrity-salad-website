Large group games

Games that still work when everyone turns up.

Large-group party games need pace, simple rules and very little downtime. The trick is usually to organise people into teams rather than asking twenty people to wait for individual turns.

Best games for large groups

For 10, 15 or 20+ people, choose formats where several people are engaged at once and where the game can survive noise, late arrivals and somebody wandering off to the kitchen.

  • Celebrity Salad: two-team celebrity guessing with repeated names across three rounds.
  • Charades: one performer, a whole team guessing.
  • Reverse charades: a whole team acts for one guesser.
  • Fishbowl / Salad Bowl: repeated prompts with progressively tighter clue rules.
  • Telephone: simple and funny with a big circle.
  • Team Pictionary: easy to scale if you have a whiteboard or large paper.
  • Quick-fire trivia: teams answer together rather than one person at a time.
  • Categories: fast verbal rounds with almost no setup.
The key to scalingMore players should usually mean bigger teams, not more individual turns. If twenty people each need a personal two-minute go, the room will spend most of the game watching.

Games for around 10 people

Ten is an ideal party-game size because you can split into two teams of five and still give everyone regular involvement.

Celebrity Salad

Ask each player to add a few celebrity names, divide into two teams and alternate timed turns. The same names return for Describe It, One Word and Act It, so the game speeds up as everyone learns the pool.

Charades relay

Use two teams and keep turns short. Award a point for each correct answer before time runs out, then switch teams immediately.

Team drawing

One person draws while the rest of their team guesses. With ten people, a 45- or 60-second timer keeps the game moving.

Games for around 15 people

With fifteen people, uneven teams are fine. The important thing is to avoid formats where the same confident players dominate every round.

Rotating clue-givers

Use a game where the clue-giver changes automatically after every turn. Celebrity Salad does this within each team so quieter players are not left out.

Reverse charades

Put most of the team on stage at once and let one player guess. It works well with a noisy crowd because there is always something happening.

Mini team quiz

Create three or four teams and keep the rounds short. Use topics the group actually enjoys instead of attempting a huge general-knowledge marathon.

Games for 20 people or more

Once the group gets beyond twenty, the host matters almost as much as the game. Give instructions once, keep a visible rhythm and avoid complicated exceptions.

Two large teams

For an energetic room, two large teams work well for guessing games. Keep the active turn short and make sure the next clue-giver is ready before the timer starts.

Multiple smaller teams

For quizzes, challenges or stations, split into three to five smaller teams. This gives more people a reason to participate at the same time.

Circle games

Telephone, rhythm games and quick categories can work without formal teams at all, provided the rule is simple enough to survive a large circle.

What to avoid with a large group

  • Long individual turns: they create too much waiting.
  • Elimination: the first people out become spectators.
  • Rules that need silence: large parties rarely stay quiet.
  • Too much equipment: cards and pieces get lost quickly.
  • One person keeping everything in their head: use a visible timer, app or score sheet.

How to host a large-group game well

  1. Explain the objective first. Tell people what they are trying to do before listing restrictions.
  2. Demonstrate one turn. A ten-second example is often clearer than another paragraph of rules.
  3. Keep teams roughly balanced. Perfect equality matters less than avoiding one tiny team.
  4. Use short turns. Thirty to sixty seconds keeps the pace high.
  5. Rotate participation. Do not let the same two extroverts take every clue-giving turn.
  6. End while it is still fun. Large-group games are better as a strong part of the evening than an obligation that drags on.

Why Celebrity-style games suit big groups

Celebrity, Fishbowl and Salad Bowl formats are naturally scalable because everyone contributes content, only one person needs to give clues at a time, and the rest of the team can guess together.

The repeated pool is also useful with a big group. Even if someone misses a clue in the first round, they gradually learn the names through other teams' turns and later rounds.
