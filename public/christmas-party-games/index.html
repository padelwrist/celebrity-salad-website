CHRISTMAS PARTY GAMES

Christmas games people will actually play.

Low prep, quick rules and enough chaos to get a room laughing without turning Christmas into compulsory organised fun.

Pick a Christmas game in 20 seconds

The best festive game depends less on Christmas and more on the room. A group of six close friends can handle a louder performance game; a mixed family or work party usually needs something easy to understand and easy to join.

12 easy Christmas party games

1. Celebrity Salad: Christmas edition

Everyone privately adds famous people or festive characters to one shared Salad, then two to four teams guess the same names across three rounds: Describe It, One Word and Act It. Each round gets more restrictive, but the names stay the same, so the room builds its own running jokes.

It works particularly well at Christmas because different generations can contribute names they know. Mix current celebrities with familiar faces, musicians, film characters and a few festive wildcards. One shared iPhone or iPad handles the timer and scoring, and the custom classic game is free. 2. Christmas Charades

Write festive films, songs, traditions, objects and characters on slips of paper. One person acts while their team guesses. Keep prompts broad enough that most of the room has a chance: “Home Alone”, “building a snowman” and “pulling a cracker” work better than obscure film trivia.

3. Festive Pictionary

Use the same style of prompts as Charades, but draw instead of act. A whiteboard is ideal, though paper works perfectly well. Give each team 45 or 60 seconds per turn and make terrible drawing part of the point.

4. Name that Christmas song

Play the first few seconds of well-known Christmas tracks and award a point for the title and another for the artist. For a mixed-age room, use a spread across decades rather than fifteen songs from one playlist.

5. Christmas Who Am I?

Give each player a famous person or festive character they cannot see. Players ask yes-or-no questions until they identify themselves. It works well while people are arriving because there is no fixed start or finish.

6. The Price Is Festive

Choose five ordinary Christmas items, such as mince pies, crackers, wrapping paper or a tub of chocolates. Teams guess the current price of each item and the closest answer wins the point. Keep the products visible or show a simple list so nobody needs specialist knowledge.

7. Christmas categories

Pick a category such as Christmas films, things on a Christmas dinner table or songs with “Christmas” in the title. Go around the room naming valid answers without repeats. Add a five-second limit when the pace needs help.

8. Festive reverse Charades

Instead of one person acting for a team, the whole team acts while one player guesses. It is useful for bigger groups because several people are involved at once and nobody has to carry the performance alone.

9. Christmas memory tray

Put 15 to 20 festive objects on a tray, show them for 30 seconds, then cover the tray. Teams write down as many as they can remember. It is simple, inclusive and useful when you need a quieter game between louder ones.

10. Two truths and a Christmas lie

Each person gives three Christmas-related statements about themselves: two true and one false. Keep it light and optional. Good prompts include unusual gifts, childhood traditions or Christmas travel rather than anything too personal.

11. Festive mini-trivia

Use short rounds rather than a 50-question marathon: five questions on films, five on music, five on food and five on general Christmas knowledge. Teams keep it social and remove the pressure of answering alone.

12. Guess the Christmas sound

Record or create simple sounds such as tearing wrapping paper, jingling keys, opening a fizzy drink, shaking a box or pulling tape from a roll. Teams identify the sound without seeing the source.

Christmas games by group size

4 to 6 people

Celebrity Salad, Charades, Pictionary and Who Am I? all work well because turns come around quickly. With Celebrity Salad, four or five submissions per player creates a fuller game.

7 to 12 people

This is the sweet spot for team guessing games. Celebrity Salad supports 4–12 players and two to four teams; three submissions each and 45- or 60-second turns are easy starting points.

More than 12 people

Choose a game that involves several people at once, such as reverse Charades, music intros or trivia. If you want Celebrity Salad, run separate Salads or short heats rather than trying to squeeze an unsupported number into one game.

How to make Christmas games work for mixed groups

  • Use shared references. Mix generations, genres and difficulty levels.
  • Avoid elimination. Nobody wants to spend Christmas watching other people play.
  • Keep alcohol separate from the rules. The game should still work for everyone in the room.
  • Use teams. They reduce pressure and help quieter players join naturally.
  • Stop while it is still fun. Two good games beat six games that feel scheduled.

Why Celebrity Salad suits Christmas parties

Christmas groups often combine people who know each other well with relatives, partners or friends who do not. Celebrity Salad gives everyone the same job, lets players contribute to the content privately and creates shared jokes quickly. The repeated names also help mixed generations because once a celebrity has appeared in Describe It, everyone has more context for One Word and Act It.
